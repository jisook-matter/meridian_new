What is Meridian

A system that detects flood risk before it reaches your streets

Meridian is the early flood warning system that protects your community by detecting flood risks hours before flooding occurs.

1

StormPuck™ water sensors and StormSight cameras are placed at high-risk locations — bridges, underpasses and stormwater pits.

2

Artificial intelligence analyses video, water levels and weather locally, at the roadside, to predict flooding.

3

Your council receives a 45–90 minute warning before flooding happens, through StormPilot.

4

Warnings go to your council and the SES, and through them to residents' phones via AusAlert. StormGate floodway signage follows from late 2027.

Result

More time to evacuate people, close roads, and protect infrastructure.

In short

StormPuck™ feels the water. StormSight sees it. StormGate closes the road. StormPilot tells you why. Between sensing and acting sits Meridian's edge intelligence — the reasoning runs at the roadside, so a storm event never depends on a working connection to a distant server.

The products

Inside the system

Three products carry Meridian from the pit to the work order. StormPuck™ senses both below and above the street, from pits and pipes to under bridges and in culverts; StormSight watches the crossings; and StormPilot turns what they find into action.

Sense · in the pit

StormPuck™: four readings from the one advanced radar/optical module

Most pit sensors return one number: how deep the water is. StormPuck™ looks into the pit. Its radar/optical sensing sees the pit itself, so a single unit tells your team four things.

1

Water level

Continuous level in the pit, so a surcharge is seen building below the street while the road is still dry.

2

Internal blockage

Flags a restricted outlet pipe when water holds in the pit longer than that pit's own history says it should.

3

External blockage

Sees the grate and inlet choking with leaves, litter or sediment before rain arrives, when clearing is cheap and safe.

4

Debris and silt

Detects debris and silt building up in the pit, so cleaning crews go where the build-up actually is.

What makes it different
  • Radar/optical sensing. It sees the condition of the pit, not just a water height from a float or pressure point.
  • One unit, four jobs. Flood warning in the wet, maintenance intelligence in the dry.
  • Part of the mesh. Every StormPuck™ is a node in the underground mesh, so neighbouring pits back each other up.
  • Simple to install. Battery-powered and IP68, fitted in about 30 minutes with no trenching or mains power.
Act · office and field

StormPilot: work management built around your drains

StormPilot is where readings become decisions and jobs. It's built for stormwater crews and emergency staff, not adapted from a generic dashboard.

  • Turns StormPuck™ and StormSight findings into prioritised jobs: which grate to clear and which pipe to jet, before which storm
  • Field crews get a clear stop list and close each job with photo evidence
  • Every alert and every job keeps a timestamped record, building the evidence asset plans and disaster recovery claims rely on
  • Operator co-sign on every public alert, with the reasoning shown
  • Designed to connect with council asset management systems
Sense · at the crossing

StormSight: self-learning AI vision

StormSight cameras read the crossings, floodways and culverts that cut communities off, and they get better at it the longer they watch.

  • Reads water depth against the depth board, plus debris, blockage and flow
  • Self-learning: each camera learns what normal looks like at its own site and sharpens with every event
  • Inference runs on the pole. Video stays on site; only the finding is sent
  • Shared learning across sites improves every camera without exporting any council's local data

Advanced property awareness for residents

Victoria already gives residents strong tools for knowing their flood risk. Meridian is designed to make them sharper, with street-level readings that reach residents through the apps they already use.

What Victoria already has

  • VicEmergency app. Residents set watch zones around their home, school or business and get warnings for those places.
  • VICSES Local Flood Guides. Minor, moderate and major flood levels for each community, and what each one means.
  • FloodZoom. The state's flood intelligence platform for agencies, combining forecasts, flood mapping, real-time river gauges and property data such as floor heights.

What Meridian adds

  • Street-level readings. Data from inside the pit network, not only river gauges, so stormwater and flash flooding that river forecasts miss can be warned on.
  • Earlier, street-specific warnings. Fed into the channels residents already use, so a watch zone names the actual street, pit or crossing.
  • Property-level context. Matched against property and floor-height data, so advice can say which homes are likely to take water.

AusAlert messages and public warnings are issued by authorised emergency agencies. Meridian supplies the trigger, the affected area and a draft message; the agency decides and sends. Connections to state platforms such as VicEmergency and FloodZoom are set up with each state's agency.

The other 350 days

Maintenance you can plan

Floods are episodic. The StormPuck™ mesh is not — it reports continuously from inside your stormwater pits every day of the year, which turns pit maintenance from a scheduled guess into a targeted response.

Inspect by condition, not calendar

  • Cyclic inspection visits every pit at the same interval, whether or not it needs attention
  • StormPuck™ flags which pits are filling, silting or draining slowly
  • Crews are sent where the data points, not down a list

Blockages found before the storm

  • A pit that drains slower than its own history is showing early blockage
  • Detected in dry weather, when clearing is cheap, safe and unhurried
  • Field-validated blockage detection in live council deployment

Evidence for asset planning

  • Multi-year condition history per pit, not per catchment
  • Identifies chronic problem assets that justify capital renewal
  • Supports asset management plans and defensible budget submissions
Why it matters

The mesh earns its keep in the dry season. A council that never floods in a given year still gets a full year of condition data on the network it is already obligated to maintain — and every blockage cleared before the storm is one less flood to warn about.

Questions & answers

The things councils ask first

What if the internet goes down?
3×
Built to stay on: three layers of redundancy and multiple mesh points. No single link, mesh point or power source carries the system alone.
Meridian is built so that no single connection failure can silence it. Detection is fully autonomous — StormPuck™ and StormSight reason locally at the roadside and keep monitoring for 7–14 days without any network at all, caching alerts and transmitting them the moment a path is restored. For getting alerts out, Meridian carries independent layers: terrestrial mobile (LTE-M / NB-IoT) as the everyday path, Starlink satellite backhaul at cluster sites, and satellite IoT as a low-bandwidth fallback that reaches orbit directly from the field. Each operates independently — losing the mobile network, or losing mains power, does not take the system offline.
Is this just another cloud system?
No. All processing happens locally on edge devices. Only encrypted alert signals leave the site. Your flood data stays in Australia.
What about privacy?
StormSight never stores raw video centrally. Alerts contain only threat-level intelligence — for example "flood warning: impassable water at Bridge St". No personal information is extracted.
How often do sensors need replacement?
Hardware typically lasts 10 years. Batteries last 5–10 years depending on surge usage. StormPuck™ is built for field servicing, so the unit can easily be swapped on site.
Can this predict extreme events, such as 100-year floods?
Yes, with lower confidence. Meridian is strongest for frequent and moderate events.
Will this replace emergency services?
No. Meridian is a tool that enhances SES / emergency response and will feed into the new AusAlert system. Humans remain in control.
